Mini Split Cost to Run Per Hour & Month

Estimate what a ductless mini-split adds to your electricity bill. Enter electrical input watts, scheduled hours, active-runtime assumption and your electricity rate. For inverter systems, measured average watts or measured kWh gives a better estimate than nameplate maximum input.

Quick answer: At 18.34¢/kWh, 1,200 W of electrical input costs about $0.22 per full-power hour. If a 1,200 W scenario is scheduled eight hours a day and averages 60% of that entered input, it costs about $31.69 per 30-day month. Your mini-split can differ substantially because inverter-driven compressors modulate output.

Why mini-split watts are not constant

Modern mini-splits commonly use inverter-driven, variable-speed compressors. The U.S. Department of Energy explains that inverter-driven heat pumps can modulate speed and capacity between low and high settings instead of operating as a simple fixed-output load. That is why multiplying maximum nameplate watts by every hour the system is switched on can overstate consumption.

Technical source: U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Pump Systems. Electricity-rate source: EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A.

How the estimate works

kWh = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × average-load percentage. Cost is kWh multiplied by the electricity rate. The state presets use June 2026 residential averages from the U.S. Energy Information Administration.

What number should you enter for watts?

  • Best: measured average watts or measured kWh over a representative day.
  • Good for scenarios: a known electrical input paired with a low and high average-load percentage.
  • Use cautiously: maximum nameplate input treated as continuous draw; variable-speed equipment may spend substantial time below maximum.
