1,500 Watt Space Heater Cost Per Hour & Month

Calculate what a portable electric space heater adds to your power bill. Start with the common 1,500 W setting or change the wattage, runtime, thermostat cycling and electricity rate to match your heater.

Quick answer: At the June 2026 U.S. residential average of 18.34¢/kWh, a 1,500 W space heater costs about $0.28 per full-power hour. Four full-power hours a day is about $33.01 per 30-day month; eight hours is about $66.02. Thermostat cycling can reduce actual use.

How the estimate works

kWh = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × active-runtime percentage. A 1,500 W heater therefore uses 1.5 kWh for each full-power hour. Cost is kWh multiplied by your electricity rate.

Rate source: EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A. The 18.34¢/kWh benchmark is the June 2026 U.S. residential average; your utility rate may differ.

Does a 1,500 watt heater always use 1,500 watts?

Not necessarily. At its 1,500 W setting, the heating element draws about that amount while actively heating, but a thermostat can switch the element off after the room reaches its set temperature. That is why scheduled hours and active heating time are separate inputs above.

What does a 750 watt heater cost?

At 18.34¢/kWh, a 750 W heater costs about $0.14 per full-power hour. If it runs eight full-power hours per day for 30 days, it uses 180 kWh and costs about $33.01.

Make the estimate more accurate

  • Use the wattage printed on your heater for the setting you actually use.
  • Use the electricity rate from your own bill when available.
  • If the thermostat cycles, measure energy use with a plug-in meter or test a lower active-runtime percentage.
