Transparent calculations

How WattCalc builds an estimate

Every calculator exposes the assumptions that drive the result. Different appliance types use different inputs because one formula is not equally useful for every load.

Steady loads

kWh = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours. Useful for loads whose power stays relatively stable.

Cycling loads

Duty cycle adjusts for thermostats, compressors or controls that do not draw full entered power continuously.

Annual-kWh loads

For refrigerators and similar equipment, annual kWh is usually more meaningful than nameplate watts.

Per-load appliances

For dryers, kWh per load maps naturally to weekly, monthly and annual use.

Electricity rates

State presets come from EIA Electric Power Monthly Table 5.6.A. The rate input is always exposed so you can replace the benchmark with your own utility rate.

Source hierarchy

  1. Government and standards sources such as EIA, DOE and ENERGY STAR.
  2. Manufacturer or model-specific specifications.
  3. Measured kWh or watts from the user's own equipment.
  4. Illustrative defaults only when no better input is available.

What a result is not

It is not a utility-bill guarantee, contractor quote, equipment warranty or financial recommendation. Weather, tariffs, taxes, cycling, efficiency, installation and behavior can change the real result.
