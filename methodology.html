WattCalc methodology

WattCalc is designed so the result can be reconstructed from the inputs on the page. We separate the arithmetic from the assumptions.

Basic electricity-cost formula

Energy (kWh) = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours. Then cost = kWh × electricity rate.

Duty cycle and variable loads

A heater, compressor or dehumidifier may be switched on for eight hours without drawing its rated power for all eight. Where useful, our calculators include an active-runtime or duty-cycle percentage. A 50% duty cycle means the entered power is treated as active for half of the scheduled time.

When watts are the wrong input

For refrigerators and similar cycling appliances, annual kWh from an EnergyGuide label or measured energy data usually produces a better estimate than nameplate watts. For dryers, kWh per load can be easier to interpret than peak heating-element wattage.

Electricity rates

State presets come from EIA Electric Power Monthly Table 5.6.A. The calculator always exposes the rate field so a user can replace the benchmark with their own utility rate.

Source hierarchy

  1. Government and standards sources such as EIA, DOE and ENERGY STAR for energy concepts and benchmark data.
  2. Manufacturer/model-specific specifications for a particular appliance.
  3. Measured kWh or watts from the user's own equipment when available.
  4. Illustrative defaults only when no better input is available; defaults are labeled as examples.

What a WattCalc result is not

It is not a utility bill forecast, contractor quote, equipment guarantee, financial recommendation or claim about every product in a category. Weather, tariffs, taxes, cycling, efficiency, installation and behavior can move the real result.

Update policy

We update benchmark data when newer source data is materially useful, and revise pages when real search queries reveal an unanswered question or an assumption that needs clarification.
